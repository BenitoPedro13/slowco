### B.3 Visual language

### D.1 OUTFIT®

Read from the saved markup and the recording:

- **What it is:** the merch store of a design studio: one page, 12 apparel items in a grid
  ($15–$36.50, each with a name like "Off by Design", "Kerned Confidence", "Command + K"), a "Why"
  blurb, a closing line ("Made to be worn. Or judged. Or both."), shipping and returns link, studio
  footer. Copy is witty and tied to the studio's own trade (typography, grids).
- **Palette:** black `#000`, cream `#ede4dd` (used as "white"), red `#ff0001` (also the custom
  cursor colour, `--cursor-color`). Three colours, no gradients seen in the tokens.
- **Interaction signals in the markup:** a bag counter built as a column of digits 0 to 9 (a
  rolling-odometer count, in the header and again in the menu), a custom cursor, Lenis smooth scroll,
  a menu overlay with Shop / Bag. Motion itself is **unverified** until we have a recording.
- **Stack:** Next.js with Turbopack, headless **Shopify** (checkout accelerators CSS present),
  Tailwind-style tokens. Close to ours; note that it uses Shopify, we don't in release 1.
- **Product imagery:** product-on-plain-ground photos, two per item (front and alternate), plus
  cream/black/red colourways: the photography does most of the work.

What to learn: a tiny, confident palette; the product name as copy; the counter and cursor as the
interactive signature; one page that stays fast. What we will **not** copy: layout, copy, product
names, the ++hellohello name or marks, assets (images in `OUTFIT_files/` are theirs and stay in
`references/`).

**Seen in the recording (OUTFIT.mov):**

- **Loader:** black screen, the word OUTFIT over a small collage of stacked photo cards, with a
  running counter (reads "085" mid-load).
- **Entry:** cream page with the wordmark OUTFIT® set huge in red across the full width, product
  photos in a row below. On scroll the letters break apart into shapes (a red arc, a T-bar, a bar),
  a scroll-driven moment on the wordmark.
- **Theme switch:** three dots at the top right swap the whole site between cream, black and red
  (the footer line "Made to be worn. Or judged. Or both." and the product page appear in cream-on-red,
  red-on-cream and cream-on-black). A full-screen red wipe with a small OUTFIT label runs between
  states.
- **Grid hover:** a red round cursor label appears on a product image; products open to a detail
  page (name large, price in red, short witty description, colour, size chips M/L/XL/2XL/S, quantity
  stepper, "Add to Bag ↗"); sold-out sizes are struck through; the second photo can be a full-bleed
  colour field.
- **Inner pages** (Privacy Policy) show an animated 3D object (a flag, a camera-like piece) beside
  the text: even legal pages carry a bit of play.

### D.3 RED AND GREEN CO.,LTD (`redandgreen.jp`)

Japanese streetwear coordinator. Seen at 2 s intervals in `redandgreen.jp.mov` (the transitions
between frames are still unseen):

- **Intro:** full-screen photo with the name in a condensed bold face, a one-line tagline and two
  pill buttons, "Sound on / Sound off": the visitor chooses audio before entering. (Autoplay audio is
  excluded by our `CLAUDE.md` §6; an explicit opt-in like this would be allowed.)
- **Cursor:** a red spiky burst (a "sun") that follows the pointer everywhere; it turns green on
  the red sections and grows over links. The strongest signature.
- **Colour takeover per section:** the whole viewport floods red, green, white or black as you
  move down; the section's photo is duotone-tinted to that colour (a green-tinted portrait, a red
  one), so the palette is carried by the images, not only by backgrounds.
- **Shards:** photos are cut into sharp polygon fragments that scatter and reassemble (ABOUT US,
  the service section); loose red/green confetti shards fly across the page. Giant marquee type
  ("SERVICE SERVICE", "BRANDS BRANDS") runs behind or through the photos.
- **Chrome:** vertical company name on the left edge, an eye icon top right (a toggle; function not
  seen), a thin inset frame around the page, the contact form in a sheet that tilts in 3D.
- **Menu:** opens as a white rounded sheet with large rows (HOME, ABOUT US, …) that highlight with
  the cursor, plus brands, latest news and access in columns.
- **Catalogue:** brand pages with line-art product thumbnails and a darkened split layout of
  photos; no price or cart (it is a B2B showcase).
- **Cautions:** very motion-heavy; the shard and colour-flood effects would need a still fallback
  and must avoid rapid red/green alternation (WCAG 2.3.1, `CLAUDE.md` §6).

### D.5 Direction proposals (Claude, to confirm with Benito)

How each reference maps to what Slow actually is (§B), kept to what the profile supports:

| Take | From | Why it fits Slow | Caution |

**Answered (Benito, 2026-11-09):** the site is **drops with an archive**, and pieces from older
drops may still be on sale. So each product has a drop (name, date) and its own availability
(`em breve`, `disponível`, `esgotado`), and the archive page lists every drop with what is still
buyable. **Photos:** Instagram photos may be used where they serve best; the people in them still
need the client's consent before launch (`08` #9).

**First moment to build (Claude's call, delegated by Benito 2026-11-09): the hero with the theme
switch.** Reasons: it fixes the palette, the type and the wordmark treatment that everything else
inherits; it is the part least dependent on missing client data (no prices or stock needed); and it
exercises the accessibility baseline (reduced motion, keyboard, contrast) early. The drop mode,
the bag counter and the cursor come after, in that order (the drop mode needs real dates and
stock, `08` #14). Next docs: `01-BRAND-BOOK.md`, then `02-VISUAL-IDENTITY.md`.

Idea from the Reels (B.6), to validate with the client: the **VIP early-access group** as a call to
action next to "Pedir no WhatsApp"; and the **globe** as the brand's recurring object (the hero
object option in the table, with the client's own artwork).
